# Space Apps 2026: Earth Analogs for Moon and Mars

## What this project is
Our team's entry for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 challenge "Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Permanent Moon Base Locations and Mars."
We score Earth sites against Moon and Mars targets (an Artemis candidate landing region at the lunar south pole, from NASA's "Artemis III candidate regions" list, and a Mars ice landing zone) on several dimensions, then show the results as an interactive map and a radar chart per site.
Deadline: Sunday Nov 15, 11:59 PM local time. Simple and working beats ambitious and broken.

## Stack (do not add anything else)
- Python 3 with pandas (and numpy, which ships with pandas), folium, plotly only.
- No JavaScript frameworks, no servers, no databases.
- The output is static HTML files in `docs/`, published by GitHub Pages.

- `data/scores_html.py`: the "Similarity scores" cards on site pages, and the MODIS citation.
- `data/sites.csv`: generated by import_sheet.py. One row per site or target. Don't hand-edit.
- `data/score.py`: computes the 0 to 1 similarity scores per dimension; checks the data.
- `data/pages.py`: shared HTML helpers and site pages. `data/topic_pages.py`: gap, task and About pages.
- `data/fetch_slope.py`: downloads Copernicus DEM GLO-30 blocks (AWS, no login) and computes slope per site into `data/copernicus_slope.csv`.
- `data/fetch_mars_slope.py`: Arcadia slope from MOLA (PDS) and Earth slopes at the same ~463 m pixel size → `data/slope_463m.csv`.
- `data/fetch_dust.py`: Earth NASA POWER AOD_55 and Mars LMD dust climatology → `data/dust_aod.csv`, `data/mars_dust.csv`.
- `data/sun_angle.py`: calculates the highest Sun angle per row (scored as `sun_max_elev_deg`).
- `data/downloads.py`: About page "How we score" + "Download our data"; build.py copies the data CSVs listed in `FILES` to `docs/data/`.
- `data/read_appeears.py`: reads AppEEARS results (MYD11A1 daily temperature → `data/modis_daily_lst.csv`, reference only; MCD43A3 albedo → `data/modis_albedo.csv`, not scored yet). Sites are matched by coordinates. AppEEARS request record and citations: `data/appeears/`.
- `data/fetch_tes_albedo.py`: Mars target albedo from the MGS TES bolometric albedo map (PDS) into `data/mars_albedo.csv`.
- `data/analog.py`: reads each site's "Analog for" text: Moon analogs are compared only with the Moon target, Mars analogs only with Mars, "both"/both-named with both. Unrecognised text shows both targets and build.py warns.
- `data/slides_page.py` (+ `slides.css`, `slides_map.png`): the 7-slide demo, `docs/slides.html` (the submission's demo link).
- `data/compare_page.py`: the Compare sites page (all sites side by side, one condition at a time).
- `data/style.css`: the look of all pages, including the map page (dark "mission control" theme); build.py copies it to `docs/style.css`.
- `data/build.py`: runs everything, writes `docs/index.html` (home page: Key findings, from `data/findings_page.py`), `docs/map.html` (map with Moon/Mars filters), a page per site, `docs/compare.html`,
  `docs/gap.html`, `docs/tasks.html` and `docs/about.html`.
- `docs/`: generated website. Do not hand-edit generated files.
- `notes/`: site write-ups and citations (plain markdown).

## CSV columns (data/sites.csv)
name, type (earth / moon_target / mars_target), lat, lon, slope_deg, temp_max_c, temp_min_c, temp_range_c, precip_mm_yr, dust_aod, sun_max_elev_deg, matches, breaks, source_links, category, analog_for, mission_tasks, key_facts, climate_notes, geology, how_well_studied
- Text columns are copied word for word from the Sheet.
- Scoring columns (slope_deg ... dust_aod) are only filled from Sheet columns with exactly those names, holding plain numbers, ground-surface values only. Never pull numbers out of text such as "<30" or "80.8 (surface, MODIS)".
- Exceptions, listed in import_sheet.py: `temp_max_c` for Earth sites comes from `data/power_skin_temps.csv` (NASA POWER TS_MAX, the maximum hourly skin temperature 2001–2025, ~50 km, made by `data/fetch_power.py`; team decision (Nov 15): max = the highest hourly ground (skin) temperature reported by the dataset in its stated period; MODIS daily `modis_daily_lst.csv` and 8-day `modis_lst.csv` are kept for reference, not scored); for targets it comes from the Targets tab column "Max ground surface temp (°C)" (LRO Diviner). Any new source must be the same kind of number and be labelled in `DIMENSION_SOURCES` in `data/scores_html.py`.
- `sun_max_elev_deg` (highest Sun angle) is calculated for every row in `data/sun_angle.py` from latitude and the axial tilts in the NASA planetary fact sheets (team-approved exception: geometry, not a measurement).
- `slope_deg` for Earth sites comes from `data/copernicus_slope.csv` (mean slope in a 15 × 15 km box, Copernicus DEM GLO-30, made by `data/fetch_slope.py`); for Malapert from the Targets tab column "Landing-area slope (°)" (Wueller et al. 2025).
- `slope_463m_deg` (Earth: Copernicus averaged to ~463 m; Arcadia: MOLA MEGDR) and `dust_aod` (Earth: NASA POWER AOD_55; Arcadia: LMD Mars dust climatology, 9.3 µm × 2.6 visible equivalent) come from `data/fetch_mars_slope.py` and `data/fetch_dust.py`. All downloaded scoring files are listed in `DOWNLOADED` in `import_sheet.py`.
- Scored so far: vs Malapert: slope (30 m), max ground temperature, Sun angle. vs Arcadia: slope (463 m), dust, Sun angle. Malapert has no dust (no air) and Arcadia no ground temperature: "no data".
- Target rows with "(reference)" in the name are not scored; they appear on the gap page only.

## Scoring rules
- Compare each Earth site only with the target(s) its "Analog for" names (team decision, Nov 15; see `data/analog.py`).
- Score each dimension separately: 1 = same value as the target, 0 = as different as anything in the dataset.
  Formula: score = 1 - abs(earth_value - target_value) / (max - min of that column across all rows), clipped to 0..1.
- Never combine dimensions into one overall number unless a teammate asks.
- A blank cell means "no data": skip it and show "no data" in charts. Never invent, estimate or fill in values.
- Show a radar chart only for sites with at least one real score; otherwise show a clear "not scored yet" note.
- The main content is the site pages, the gap page ("Where Earth falls short") and the task table. The task table is team judgment, not computed, and must say so.
- Every site page must show the "matches" and "breaks" text, plus a fixed note that Earth cannot reproduce lunar or Martian gravity, vacuum or thin atmosphere, or radiation. The note names only the site's own body (Moon: gravity, vacuum, radiation; Mars: gravity, thin atmosphere, radiation; both: the full note), and Moon-only / Mars-only pages show nothing about the other body (`pages.limits_note`, `scores_html.source_for`).

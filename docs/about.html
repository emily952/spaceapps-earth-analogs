About & credits

What this is

Earth places that resemble parts of the Moon's south pole (Malapert Massif) and a Martian ice plain (Arcadia Planitia): what matches, where each falls short, and which mission tests each could support. We also score individual conditions from 0 to 1 with 1 - |Earth value - target value| / (largest - smallest value in our dataset), but only where we have exact, comparable measurements; scores are never added together. Missing data shows as "no data"; we never estimate.

Limits of any Earth analog: No place on Earth can reproduce lunar or Martian gravity, the Moon's vacuum or Mars' thin atmosphere, or their radiation environment.

Use of AI

AI tools helped with this project's research and coding (coding: Claude Code and Cursor). Team members verified the sources and reviewed the code.

How we score

For each condition separately: score = 1 − |Earth value − target value| / (largest − smallest value in our data), kept between 0 and 1. 1 = same as the target; 0 = as different as anything in our data.

Example (slope): Harrat Khaybar and Umm Jirsan lava tube, Saudi Arabia is 3.78°; Malapert Massif is 5.6°. The gap is 1.82. The widest spread in our data is 0.45 to 11.04° = 10.59. Score = 1 − 1.82 / 10.59 = 0.83.

Conditions scored so far:

  • Slope: Earth: Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (~30 m pixels; wider east-west near the poles), mean slope over a 15 × 15 km box around the site, the size of an Artemis III landing region. At this pixel size, measurement noise adds about 0.5–1° on flat ground. Moon: Wueller et al. 2025, Malapert candidate landing area, 5.6° ± 2.0° (their map resolution is not given in the table we used, so the two may not be measured at the same pixel size).
  • Max temperature: Earth: MODIS Aqua land surface temperature (MYD11A2), hottest 8-day average of clear-sky ~1:30 pm readings in 2025, one 1 km pixel. Moon: LRO Diviner, Malapert ridge top.
  • Highest Sun angle: Calculated from each place's latitude (one point) and axial tilt (NASA planetary fact sheets); flat horizon assumed, hills and slopes ignored. Earth: tilt 23.44°, so even at the poles the highest Sun is 23.44°. Moon: tilt 1.54°, so no Earth site can match Malapert's low Sun. Mars: tilt 25.19°. Why it matters: at the Moon's south pole the Sun stays near the horizon, so shadows are very long and crews and cameras look almost straight into the Sun. Glare and deep shadow make landing, driving and finding hazards harder: a real problem for Artemis astronauts and rovers.

Scores are never added into one overall number. Adding or removing a site can shift every score a little, because the spread comes from our own data. A condition without exact, comparable values on both sides shows "no data": we never estimate.

Ground temperatures: MODIS (1 km)

ORNL DAAC. 2018. MODIS and VIIRS Land Product Subsets RESTful Web Service. https://doi.org/10.3334/ORNLDAAC/1600 . Product MYD11A2 v6.1 (Wan, Hook, Hulley).

Product MYD11A2 v6.1 (MODIS Aqua, 8-day land surface temperature, band LST_Day_1km), year 2025, accessed 2026-11-15. For each site we keep the hottest 8-day average of clear-sky daytime readings (Aqua passes about 1:30 pm local time) in the one 1 km pixel at its coordinates. Service: modis.ornl.gov

We first used NASA POWER (~50 km grid cell) and switched to MODIS because it is the smallest Earth temperature data on the challenge Resources list that covers all 12 sites without a login. The POWER download is kept in our repository for reference; it is not scored.

What this is

Earth places that resemble parts of the Moon's south pole (Malapert Massif) and a Martian ice plain (Arcadia Planitia): what matches, where each falls short, and which mission tests each could support. We also score individual conditions from 0 to 1 with 1 - |Earth value - target value| / (largest - smallest value in our dataset), but only where we have exact, comparable measurements; scores are never added together. Missing data shows as "no data"; we never estimate.

Limits of any Earth analog: No place on Earth can reproduce lunar or Martian gravity, the Moon's vacuum or Mars' thin atmosphere, or their radiation environment.

How we score

For each condition separately: score = 1 − |Earth value − target value| / (largest − smallest value in our data), kept between 0 and 1. 1 = same as the target; 0 = as different as anything in our data.

Example (slope): Harrat Khaybar and Umm Jirsan lava tube, Saudi Arabia is 3.78°; Malapert Massif is 5.6°. The gap is 1.82. The widest spread in our data is 0.45 to 11.04° = 10.59. Score = 1 − 1.82 / 10.59 = 0.83.

Conditions scored so far:

  • Slope: Earth: Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (~30 m pixels; wider east-west near the poles), mean slope over a 15 × 15 km box around the site, the size of a NASA Artemis candidate landing region. At this pixel size, measurement noise adds about 0.5–1° on flat ground. Moon: Wueller et al. 2025, Malapert candidate landing area, 5.6° ± 2.0° (their map resolution is not given in the table we used, so the two may not be measured at the same pixel size).
  • Slope (463 m pixels): Earth: Copernicus DEM GLO-30 heights averaged into ~463 m blocks, mean slope over a 15 × 15 km box. Mars: MOLA laser-altimeter map (MEGDR, 128 pixels per degree, ~463 m), same box; between laser tracks the map is filled in, so it is smoother than the ground. Both sides use the same pixel size, because bigger pixels make slopes look gentler.
  • Max temperature: Earth: MODIS Aqua land surface temperature (MYD11A1 v6.1, via NASA AppEEARS), hottest good-quality daily reading in 2025 (Aqua, ~1:30 pm local), one 1 km pixel. Moon: LRO Diviner, Malapert ridge top.
  • Dust (AOD): Earth: NASA POWER aerosol optical depth at 0.55 µm (all aerosols: dust, smoke, salt), 2001–2025 average, ~1° (~100 km) grid. Mars: LMD Mars dust climatology from orbiter measurements (Montabone et al. 2015), Mars Years 24–36 average, 3° (~180 km) grid, for the full local air column at Arcadia's surface pressure (~830 Pa average, from the Mars Climate Database; not the 610 Pa reference), 9.3 µm dust optical depth × 2.6 for the visible-light equivalent (LMD's conversion). Different wavelengths and grid sizes, so treat the match as approximate.
  • Highest Sun angle: Calculated from each place's latitude (one point) and axial tilt (NASA planetary fact sheets); flat horizon assumed, hills and slopes ignored. Earth: tilt 23.44°, so nowhere on Earth does the Sun's highest point of the year stay below 23.4°. Moon: tilt 1.54°, so no Earth site can match Malapert's low Sun. Mars: tilt 25.19°. Why it matters: at the Moon's south pole the Sun stays near the horizon, so shadows are very long and crews and cameras look almost straight into the Sun. Glare and deep shadow make landing, driving and finding hazards harder: a real problem for Artemis astronauts and rovers.

Scores are never added into one overall number. Adding or removing a site can shift every score a little, because the spread comes from our own data. A condition without exact, comparable values on both sides shows "no data": we never estimate.

Download our data

The exact numbers behind every score (CSV files; open in any spreadsheet):

  • sites.csv: Every Earth site and target: coordinates, scored values and all text
  • scores.csv: Every score shown on the site: one row per Earth site and the target it is compared with
  • modis_daily_lst.csv: Hottest daily MODIS temperature per site, with days kept and dropped
  • copernicus_slope.csv: Slope per site (1, 5 and 15 km boxes) from Copernicus DEM GLO-30
  • slope_463m.csv: Slope at ~463 m pixels: Earth (Copernicus, averaged) and Mars (MOLA)
  • dust_aod.csv: Earth dust/haze optical depth per site (NASA POWER AOD_55)
  • mars_dust.csv: Mars dust optical depth at Arcadia, one row per Mars Year, plus the average

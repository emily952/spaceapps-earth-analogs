Where Earth falls short

Some Moon and Mars conditions have no match anywhere on Earth. Tests that depend on them need laboratory chambers or other methods, not field sites.

Coldest surfaces

Earth's coldest measured surface (about −98 °C, East Antarctic Plateau) is colder than sunlit lunar polar spots, but about 135 °C warmer than the Moon's shadowed craters.

Lowest Sun

At Malapert Massif the Sun never climbs higher than about 5.5° above the horizon: it skims along the edge of the sky, casting very long shadows. On Earth, even at the poles, the Sun reaches 23.4° in summer, because Earth is tilted 23.44° but the Moon only 1.5°. Our lowest site, East Antarctic Plateau cold spots, gets 33.1°. Low-Sun lighting for cameras, solar panels and shadow navigation has to be tested in a lab or simulated.

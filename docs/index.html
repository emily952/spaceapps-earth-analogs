Where on Earth can we rehearse the Moon and Mars?

Before astronauts land near the Moon's south pole or on Mars, crews, rovers and instruments practise in similar places on Earth. We compared 12 Earth sites with two targets, one condition at a time, so mission planners and field-test teams can see which site to use for which test, and where no place on Earth can help.

Moon: Malapert Massif

An Artemis candidate landing region near the lunar south pole.

Closest Earth site for each condition:

  • Slope: Harrat Khaybar and Umm Jirsan lava tube (3.78°) vs 5.6°; score 0.83
  • Max temperature: Lonar Crater (51.95 °C) vs 47 °C; score 0.95
  • Highest Sun angle: East Antarctic Plateau cold spots (33.07°) vs 5.54°; score 0.67

Mars: Arcadia Planitia

A flat, ice-rich plain proposed for future landings.

Closest Earth site for each condition:

  • Slope (463 m pixels): Lut Desert (Dasht-e Lut) (0.44°) vs 0.36°; score 0.98
  • Dust (AOD): Dallol (0.4) vs 0.377; score 0.96
  • Highest Sun angle: Western Qaidam Basin (75.4°) and Río Tinto (Peña de Hierro) (75.72°) vs 67.99°; score 0.91

No single best analog

6 different sites come out on top across the conditions we score. That's why we never add scores into one ranking: pick the site that matches the test you need to run.

Where Earth falls short

  • Low Sun: nowhere on Earth does the Sun's highest point of the year stay below 23.4°; at Malapert Massif it never rises above 5.5°.
  • Cold: Earth's coldest surface (about -98 °C) is about 135 °C warmer than the Moon's shadowed craters (-233 °C).
  • Everywhere: No place on Earth can reproduce lunar or Martian gravity, the Moon's vacuum or Mars' thin atmosphere, or their radiation environment.
